# What a good creator profile looks like vs a bad one

Derived from the AI + Marketing creator study: 1,526 creator accounts (2k–150k followers) on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn and X, plus 23,120 individual YouTube/LinkedIn/X posts (Apify, Oct 2026).

**Why this file exists:** the creator you validate may not be in AI/marketing, so the study's absolute ER levels may not apply. The *shape* of a healthy account does carry over: how consistent the posts are, how many followers actually see them, how engagement reacts to posting frequency, which formats lift reach. Use this file as the main yardstick. Use `benchmarks.md` (AI-niche percentiles) only when the creator is in AI / marketing / tech / SaaS.

## How the groups were defined

- **Bad** = any of: average engagement ≥ 5× median (viral skew), best post ≥ 50% of sample engagement (one-hit), 21+ posts/week with ER < 0.1% (bulk posting), or median views÷followers in the bottom 10% of the platform tier (dead or bought followers).
- **Good** = none of the above, avg÷median < 2.5, best post < 30% of engagement, median ER at or above the platform-tier median, active in the last 30 days, 10+ posts sampled.
- Everyone else = middle. Counts: Instagram 58 good / 54 bad, TikTok 29 / 86, YouTube 57 / 68, LinkedIn 130 / 35, X 128 / 94.

## 1. Good and bad accounts get the same engagement per view. What differs is reach to their own followers.

| Platform | Median views÷followers, good | bad | ER by views, good | bad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 0.22 | 0.03 | 2.6% | 2.9% |
| TikTok | 0.34 | 0.06 | 8.3% | 8.0% |
| YouTube | 0.18 | 0.02 | 2.1% | 1.7% |
| X | 0.35 | 0.05 | 3.4% | 2.6% |

People who see a bad account's post react to it about as much as anyone else's. The difference is that most of its followers never see the post: they are inactive, bought, or left over from one viral moment. **Median views÷followers is the best single fake-audience test.** Below ~0.05 is a bad-profile signature on every video platform.

## 2. Consistency is the clearest dividing line

| Platform | Avg÷median engagement, good | bad | Best post's share of sample engagement, good | bad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 1.3× | 6.9× | 13% | 40% |
| TikTok | 1.6× | 9.1× | 17% | 48% |
| YouTube | 1.4× | 5.2× | 20% | 54% |
| LinkedIn | 1.3× | 3.5× | 15% | 30% |
| X | 1.2× | 3.2× | 13% | 31% |

169 of 1,526 accounts (11%) had average engagement ≥ 5× their median. On TikTok it was 65 of 243 (27%). Healthy accounts sit at 1.2–1.6×. Rule of thumb: **≤ 2× is healthy, 2–3× needs a look, 3–5× is a warning, ≥ 5× is a no-go.**

## 3. Posting frequency vs engagement

Median ER relative to same-platform, same-tier peers (1.0 = typical), by posts per week. "Bad %" = share of accounts in that band with a bad profile.

| Posts/week | Instagram | TikTok | YouTube | LinkedIn | X |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 0.5 | 1.79 (36% bad) | 0.59 (44%) | 1.55 (20%) | **3.05** (3%) | – |
| 0.5–1 | **2.15** (11%) | 3.51 (50% bad: viral-driven) | **1.49** (24%) | 2.07 (6%) | – |
| 1–3 | 1.36 (20%) | **1.22** (30%) | 0.79 (27%) | 1.40 (4%) | 1.06 |
| 3–7 | 1.17 (13%) | 1.09 (27%) | 0.74 (30%) | 0.89 (4%) | 0.99 |
| 7–14 | 0.58 (31%) | 0.68 (49%) | 0.83 | 0.59 (7%) | **1.20** (16%) |
| 14–21 | 0.70 | 0.70 (48%) | 0.03 | 0.17 | **1.22** (17%) |
| 21–50 | 0.08 (82% bad) | 0.81 | – | 0.10 (94% bad) | 0.86 (29%) |
| 50+ | – | – | – | 0.00 | 0.61 (31%) |

Read it as:
- **Sweet spots:** Instagram 0.5–7/week, TikTok 1–7, YouTube ≤ 1 (long-form), LinkedIn ≤ 3, X 7–21.
- **Danger zones:** Instagram/TikTok/LinkedIn above 7/week, YouTube above 3, X above 50. Very high volume plus low ER = automation or content farm.
- Very low frequency on TikTok/Instagram with high ER usually means a few viral posts, not a loyal audience. Check the consistency numbers.
- **Within a creator** (post level, YouTube/LinkedIn): a post published < 6h after the previous one gets 0.68× (YouTube) and 0.74× (LinkedIn) of the creator's median. A YouTube video 3+ days after the last one gets 1.2×. If a creator's engagement per post falls in their busy weeks, they are spreading the same audience thinner. Your sponsored post would be one of many.

## 4. Formats that lift or drag (engagement vs the same creator's median)

| Platform | Lifts | Drags |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | long-form 1.22×; 8–15 min 1.3×, 15–30 min 1.57×, 30 min+ 1.91× | Shorts 0.64×; 6+ hashtags 0.57× |
| LinkedIn | multi-image 1.72×; single image 1.19×; 80+ words 1.14× | quote/reshare 0.52×; polls 0.53×; external link in post 0.70×; < 15 words 0.62× |
| X | video 1.29×; image 1.22×; 41–80 words 1.21× | quote tweets 0.77×; < 15 words 0.88× |
| Instagram (account level) | ≥ 50% reels → 1.2× peer ER | mostly static images → 0.5× |
| TikTok (account level) | video-only → 1.18× | slideshow-heavy → 0.75× |

Use this two ways: (1) explain *why* a creator's numbers look the way they do, and (2) write the brief. Ask for the format that works best for this creator, not the one the brand prefers.

## 5. Account-type signals

- Instagram business accounts: 0.38× peer ER vs 1.62× for personal accounts. Many "creators" are brands or agencies in disguise. Check the bio and content for first-person voice.
- TikTok business accounts: 53% bad profiles vs 32% for personal ones.
- Creators who post the same content on several platforms are rarely strong on X (7% good vs 31% for single-platform) or TikTok (6% vs 13%). 78 of 135 multi-platform creators clearly posted identical content across platforms. If a creator sends several links, find their *native* platform: the one where their median, not average, is best.

## 6. Comments as a quality signal

The share of engagement that comes from comments is higher in good accounts on Instagram (16% vs 9.5%), YouTube (10.6% vs 6.6%) and X (9.1% vs 7.4%). Likes are the cheapest signal to fake; comments, shares and saves cost the audience effort. TikTok is the exception (comments ~2% of engagement everywhere), so there, look at shares + saves instead.

## 7. Sponsored content in the sample

Disclosed sponsored posts were rare: 3.8% of YouTube posts, 0.4% of LinkedIn and X. Where they existed, sponsored posts performed about the same as the creator's typical post (YouTube 1.37×, partly because sponsors buy long-form; X ~1.0×; LinkedIn 0.84×, n = 29). Bad YouTube profiles had more ads (5.2% of posts) than good ones (3.1%). The practical rule is **check this creator's own sponsored posts against their own median**. That is the best predictor of what your post will do. If they have no past sponsored posts, say the use-case score is unproven.

## Good profile checklist (any niche)

- Avg÷median ≤ 2×, best post < 25% of sample engagement, few posts below half the median.
- Median views ≥ ~0.15× followers (video platforms).
- Posting frequency in the platform sweet spot, with no drop in per-post engagement in busy weeks.
- Comments/shares/saves make up a healthy share of engagement. Comments include real questions, and the creator answers them.
- A person, not a brand: first-person voice, a consistent face and voice.
- Past sponsored posts perform near their own median and show a real use case.
- A flat or rising engagement trend over the last 30 days vs before.

## Bad profile signatures

- One or two viral posts carry the average (≥ 5× median), or one post holds half of all engagement.
- Tiny views vs followers (< 0.05×): inactive or bought followers.
- High volume (Instagram/LinkedIn 21+/week) with ER near zero: automation or content farm.
- Comments are mostly emoji, one-word CTA keywords ("GUIDE"), the same accounts every time (pod), or spam.
- Business/brand account presenting as a creator; heavy identical cross-posting.
- Many ads (> 30% of recent posts), or a recent deal with a direct competitor.
